# Start Here: What "Closing the Books" Actually Means

Somewhere around December 27 every year, tiny-business owners get a vague feeling of dread. Not because anything is wrong, but because a quiet question surfaces: *"Am I supposed to be doing something with those bank statements right now?"*

Yes. And it's smaller than you fear.

## What you're actually doing

"Closing the books" for a tiny business is really just four jobs wearing a scary name:

1. **Prove your numbers are true.** Your bank balance and your records agree, to the penny, for the whole year. (The [December reconciliation ritual](01-bank-reconciliation-december-ritual.md).)
2. **Sort the year's money into labeled buckets.** Income and expenses grouped into categories that mean something. ([Categorizing](02-categorizing-a-year-of-transactions.md).)
3. **Handle the special items.** Miles driven, any contractors you paid, any employees. ([Mileage](03-mileage-totals.md), [1099 prep](04-1099-prep.md), [W-2 vs. contractor notes](05-w2-vs-contractor-notes.md).)
4. **Store it all safely, then let it go.** Archive the year so next January starts clean. ([What to archive](06-what-to-archive.md).)

That's it. Every checklist item in this guide belongs to one of those four jobs.

## The honest promise of this guide

- It explains **mechanics**: what a reconciliation is, what a 1099 envelope looks like, why mileage logs matter.
- It does **not** tell you what to file, deduct, or owe. That's tax territory, and tax territory needs a professional who knows your actual situation. When a topic dips toward "ask a pro," you'll see a marker like this: **→ taxes: ask a pro.**

## Your realistic time budget

For a small side business, budget:

- **First year:** 4–6 hours total (you're building the habit and maybe fixing last year's drift)
- **After that:** 2–3 hours, most of it staring at your screen waiting for your bank to finish loading

Split it across two evenings in early January and it's painless. Doing it the last week of December is more dramatic and equally fine.

## A map of the whole ritual

```
Week 1:  Gather statements, do the December reconciliation, sweep the
         other 11 months of reconciliations if you haven't been doing them
Week 2:  Categorize everything; total mileage; flag contractors and
         employees; snapshot your archive; print and tear up the checklist
```

## What "closed" feels like when you're done

You can answer these three questions in about thirty seconds:

1. What did the business **take in** this year?
2. What did it **spend** this year, and on what?
3. Where does every **piece of paper** about this year live?

If you can answer those, your books are closed. Everything after this page is just *how*.
