# W-2 vs. Contractor Notes: Who's Who, Kept Straight

You don't need an employment-law seminar. You need a note in your file that captures *how you and someone else understood the arrangement*, made at the time of the arrangement. This chapter explains the two basic shapes of "paying a human" and — this is the whole trick — gets you to write the distinguishing facts down **while the facts are vivid**.

## The two shapes of paying a person

### Shape A: an employee (W-2 territory)

Someone you told *when, where, and how* to work — on your schedule, under your direction, maybe with your equipment. Payroll has machinery (withholding, depositing, filing payroll forms) and machinery has deadlines. **→ taxes: ask a pro** before you employ anyone; the setup is 100% a do-it-right-first situation.

Years-end relevant: if you *had* employees this year, your December note to self is: payroll records are tidy, final pay run is closed, and the year's payroll numbers are printed/archived. That's it — the machinery did the rest during the year.

### Shape B: a contractor / freelancer (1099 territory, [Chapter 4](04-1099-prep.md))

You contracted for a *result* — "design me a cover," "fix the shop's signs" — and they decided their own schedule, tools, and method. You just paid them.

Years-end relevant: the W-9s and payment list from Chapter 4. Nothing else, unless your situation says otherwise (**→ taxes: ask a pro** for any ruling).

### The line between them is famously gray

Independence looks case-by-case: how much control, whose tools, ongoing vs. project-based, exclusivity. Everyone spins somewhere on the continuum. It is exactly the kind of question where a wrong-but-confident guess costs more than a half-hour with a professional before you make the arrangement: **→ taxes: ask a pro.**

## What to write down (the actual skill)

For every human you paid this year — or intend to pay next year — a five-line note, saved with your year-end records:

```
Person: Priya V.
What: 3 product photos for the eBay listings
When: paid Feb 14, 2025 (see Books/2025/Pro photos)
Shape: contractor — hired for deliverable, set own hours/tools
Theirs: W-9 on file (yes/no), address on file (yes/no)
```

Five lines, written while memory is fresh, converts a January "wait, who is that Venmo payment?" into a closed file. It also does something subtler: it forces you to consciously *choose* a shape (employee vs. contractor) at hiring time rather than discovering an accidental choice at audit time.

## Two notes worth pinning next year

- **Before hiring anyone in the new year:** write the 5-line note *first*. If the employee/contractor shape feels uncertain, resolve that with a pro before the first payment, not after.
- **If you employ anyone at all:** keep payroll fully in its machinery all year and only do the December tidy; don't be piecing a payroll ledger together in January.

---

**Done when:** every person you paid services to this year has a short note with name, amount, shape, and W-9-on-file status — and the ones that looked even slightly employee-shaped have a **→ taxes: ask a pro** flag attached rather than a shrug.
