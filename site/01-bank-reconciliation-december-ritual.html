The December Bank Reconciliation Ritual

Reconciliation sounds like something accountants do at midnight. In practice it's a matching game: your records say you made one set of transactions; the bank says you made the same transactions. Make them agree.

Why bother? Because every number you ever report about your business — income, expenses, anything — is only trustworthy if it agrees with the bank. Reconciling is how you prove agreement instead of assuming it.

The core idea in one paragraph

Your books (spreadsheet, notebook, software) record transactions when they happen. The bank records them when they clear. Those two moments differ by days, and occasionally a fee or interest line sneaks into the bank statement that your books never heard about. Reconciliation finds and fixes those gaps so the two views end at the same balance.

What you'll need

  • A December bank statement (PDF or paper)
  • Your business records for December
  • 30–60 minutes and one uninterrupted playlist
  • A highlighter or a "checked" column, depending on paper vs. spreadsheet

If you run the business through a checking account (you should — mixing personal and business money is the #1 thing that makes this harder), this is a one-account job. Two or three accounts? Same ritual, per account.

The ritual, step by step

1. Get the statement's ending balance

Write it down. This is the bank number — the truth the ritual serves.

2. Match, line by line

For each transaction, find its twin in your records and mark both copies as matched. Most months this is boring, which means it's working.

The pairs that usually don't match:

  • Checks that someone hasn't cashed yet ("outstanding checks")
  • Card purchases from December 27–31 that post in January
  • The bank's own fees — service charges, wire fees, interest — which are often invisible in your records until now

3. Handle what didn't match

You have exactly three categories of stray transactions, and it's worth naming them while you work:

  • In books, not on statement → almost always timing. It cleared after the statement period. Carry it to next month's reconciliation; do not delete it.
  • On statement, not in books → usually a fee or adjustment. Add it to your records with a category like "Bank fees." If it's something else — a duplicate, a charge you don't recognize — investigate today; unexplained charges are the actual reason reconciliation exists.
  • Amounts that differ → find which record is right. If it's your typo, fix your record. → taxes: ask a pro if it ever looks like the bank's error that's favorable to you and large; document it either way.

4. Do the tiny math check

This is the classic "balancing the difference" trick, cleaned up:


Bank ending balance
 − outstanding (uncleared) withdrawals
 + outstanding deposits not yet on the statement
 + bank fees/interest you haven't recorded yet (moved to your records)
 should equal your records' ending balance

Or run it the other direction — whatever language your brain speaks. When the two numbers land on the same penny, December is reconciled.

5. The December bonus step: sweep the whole year

If you reconciled monthly all year (or never did), December is when you sweep the remaining months. The method never changes — it's the same statement-by-statement matching game, just more rounds of it. A month with a tiny unexplained difference you cannot find after twenty patient minutes is worth a note in your archive file; accountants call it a reconciling item, a normal human calls it "the $2.40 ghost," and both are honest about it. → taxes: ask a pro if a month's difference is big, not small — that's a different conversation.

If you use software that "reconciles for you"

Great — use its reconciliation screen and run the same matching logic inside it. The ritual doesn't disappear; the tool just keeps the math for you. You still judge what each stray transaction is.

Red flags worth pausing on

  • A reconciliation that's been wrong for months and you've been carrying the difference forward — stop; reconcile those months fully before December.
  • Any subscription card charge you don't recognize — cancel now, think about it never.
  • A transaction you're tempted to "just leave" — leaving it is how a $3 ghost becomes a $300 ghost.

Done when: bank ending balance, records ending balance, and your tiny math check all agree, for December and every other month of the year.
